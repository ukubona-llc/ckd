Specific Aims

Title: AI-Augmented Mapping of HIV-Associated Chronic Kidney Disease in Uganda: A Pilot “Renal Digital Twin” Framework

Significance

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) in sub-Saharan Africa is rising at the intersection of infectious disease and non-communicable risk factors. In Uganda, improved HIV survival has exposed a growing burden of HIV-associated nephropathy (HIVAN), yet the epidemiology remains fragmented across published studies and Ministry of Health reports.

The central gap is not lack of data, but lack of a continuous synthesis system capable of integrating evidence into actionable national insight. Current systematic reviews are static, slow, and disconnected from policy use.

Key Gap: No infrastructure exists to continuously synthesize HIV–CKD evidence into planning-ready outputs.

Addressing this gap is essential for aligning clinical evidence with national decision-making and resource allocation.

Innovation

This project introduces a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL), AI-augmented evidence synthesis system that transforms the systematic review into a continuously updating platform.

  • Integrates peer-reviewed and “grey” data sources
  • Applies retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for structured extraction
  • Uses human validation to ensure clinical accuracy
  • Outputs a dynamic geospatial visualization (“Renal Digital Twin”)
Conceptual Advance: From static review → continuously updating national evidence system

Specific Aims

Aim 1: Conduct an AI-augmented systematic synthesis of HIV-associated CKD in Uganda.
Outcome: Validated dataset + identification of data gaps (“loss in measurement”).
Aim 2: Develop a geospatial “Renal Digital Twin” prototype for risk visualization.
Outcome: Interactive dashboard for policy and planning use.

Impact

This project establishes a scalable framework for integrating clinical evidence into national health planning. By aligning with priorities of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, it provides a pathway for future expansion into broader disease mapping systems.

Long-term Vision: A national “Digital Twin” infrastructure for disease surveillance and planning.
